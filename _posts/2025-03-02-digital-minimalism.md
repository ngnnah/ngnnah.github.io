Digital minimalism isn't another aesthetic for your Instagram. It's a rebellion against the attention economy—a deliberate reclaiming of mental space in an increasingly crowded digital landscape.

## The Algorithm Diet

Cal Newport calls it "focusing your online time on a small number of carefully selected activities that strongly support things you value, then happily missing everything else."

I call it my Algorithm Diet: consuming only what nourishes.

## My Digital Detox Protocol

```
function implementMinimalism() {
  removeNoiseGenerators();
  createIntentionalSpaces();
  optimizeForDeepThinking();
  return enhancedClarity;
}
```

What this looks like in practice:

- **Input curation** ⟶ Newsletters over social feeds, books over endless browsing
- **Attention firewalls** ⟶ No notifications before 10am or after 8pm
- **Workflow simplification** ⟶ Three essential tools, not thirty "productivity" apps
- **Time batching** ⟶ Email twice daily, not every seven minutes

## The Unexpected Results

The space between thoughts has expanded. Ideas connect more naturally when not constantly interrupted by digital confetti.

Most surprising? I enjoy technology _more_. Like a good espresso versus bottomless diner coffee—the experience is richer for being bounded.

## The Ongoing Experiment

This isn't about digital asceticism. It's about intentional digital architecture—designing your information environment rather than inheriting it from algorithms.

My challenge: What would happen if you treated your attention with the same care as your most elegant code? What unnecessary processes would you terminate?

_I'm curious: what's one digital habit you've optimized away?_
