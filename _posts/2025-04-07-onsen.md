The best hours I have spent travelling, I spent naked. Not at a temple. Not at a tasting menu. Sitting in volcanic water with strangers.

The ritual is simple enough to write on a napkin. Leave the city noise at the door. Wash. Get into 42°C mineral water until you can't stand it. Move to the 10°C plunge until you can't stand that either. Repeat.

You wash *before* you get in, which tells you the bath is for something other than getting clean. There's a word for the state on the other side of it: *totonou*.

The pattern is older and wider than any one country. Romans ran caldarium to tepidarium to frigidarium. Finns run sauna to ice and call the aftermath *löyly*. Hot, cold, repeat, arrived at by people with no reason to compare notes.

What surprised me was the social part. Clothes off, the markers go with them. The man beside me has no idea I work remotely. The grandmother across from me explains how she pickles yuzu. A tattooed foreigner and a conservative local end up in conversation, which in that room is not nothing. In a world sorted by algorithm, an hour of accidental equality is worth sitting in hot water for.

Last winter, under stars and steam, I worked something out about myself. I'd spent years optimizing everything: work, fitness, relationships. Some things want oscillation instead. Hot, then cold, then hot again, on purpose.

Bruce Lee said "be water, my friend." After enough hours in volcanic water, I've come around to the reciprocal: be my friend, water.
