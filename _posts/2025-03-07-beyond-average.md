> "Average" is a statistical fiction we cling to, promising clarity in chaos.

Yet life thrives not in the middle, but at edges where the exceptional happens. You aren't average—your path is uniquely yours, shaped by choices that statistics can't capture.

The "average person" exists only in abstraction. No average Roman citizens walked ancient streets; senators and slaves inhabited different worlds. Evolution favors not the mean but the outlier—_the first bird flew because it broke from normal_.

## Two Modern Myths ✘✘

1. That average defines us
2. That being a generalist means being mediocre

Both are false.

## The Jack of All Trades 🔄

The jack-of-all-trades is the **polished stone**—smoothed by diverse experience, rolling effortlessly through changing landscapes.

Like the adaptable coyote who thrives anywhere, the generalist possesses a rare versatility that specialists lack. Leonardo da Vinci's genius wasn't in mastering one discipline but in connecting many.

Here lies the paradox:

> **Rejecting "average" means embracing the generalist—itself an extraordinary path**

Today's world rewards neither the median nor the over-specialized, but those who adapt across domains. The generalist isn't average; they're remarkable precisely because they remain in motion.

## Ancient Wisdom, Modern Life 🌊

Stoics taught self-mastery in all situations.  
Taoists showed how water's flexibility becomes its strength.

Both understood what we often forget: true resilience comes not from finding your center, but from flowing between possibilities.

---

Abandon both illusions. Instead, become the polished stone—smooth enough to roll in any direction, strong enough to endure impact.

_In a world of jagged edges, the ones who can adapt will always travel furthest._
